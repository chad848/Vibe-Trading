# Founding Garages

The owner-run playbook for seeding supply before Sell My Project goes public.
Companion to `ROAD-TO-NINE.md` (the engineering punch list) and `test-site/`.

Rendered version: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/9c50ba67-c4cd-4e30-b88a-189b8ad98d1d

**Goal: 25 hand-picked garages, invited personally, live before anyone else
sees the site.** Not 250 signups — 25 real ones you could name from memory.

## 01 — Why this is first, not last

A marketplace with no supply isn't a small marketplace, it's nothing. A buyer
who lands on an empty bench doesn't come back later — they conclude the site is
dead, and they're right. And it has the longest lead time on the board: code
ships in a day; a builder who trusts you enough to put his project on a new
site takes a week of conversation.

This is also the one job the owner is uniquely built for. Not a founder
cold-calling about a platform — a guy with a Blazer asking other guys with
projects to show theirs. It doesn't scale, which is exactly why it's a moat.

## 02 — Who, and who not

Filter: **an unfinished project he's proud of, and parts piling up in the
corner.** A finished show car has nothing to post; no project means nothing to
sell.

| Tier | Who | Target | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | People who already know you — bought from, sold to, wrenched with | 10 | Your phone. Start here; the first five yeses make the next twenty askable |
| 2 | The local scene — cars & coffee, swap meets | 6 | Weekend show circuit, in person, phone out with the test site open |
| 3 | One-man shops with a following (2k–30k, real progress not resellers) | 6 | Niche hashtags; shops tagged in builds you already follow |
| 4 | Forum build-thread regulars | 3 | Marque forums. Read the thread first, reference it specifically |

**Not on the list:** dealers, flippers, parts resellers, anyone who asks about
volume before they ask about the site. They fill the bench fastest and poison
it permanently — one SKU dump turns a garage into an eBay page, and you can't
un-ring it.

## 03 — The pitch

Three versions, same spine: *show your build, sell your take-offs, get in
before anyone else.* Shape, not a script to read.

**The DM.** Open by naming a specific detail of his build. Then: your garage,
your builds, a bench for the parts you pull off along the way — not a
classifieds site; people follow the build, so when you sell it or a part off
it, the audience is already there. Close: hand-picking the first 25 garages
before it goes public, ten minutes to set up, costs nothing, sellers never pay.

**In person (30 seconds).** "You selling that? … Nobody ever is. But you're
pulling parts off it — what happens to them?" Let him answer (shelf, Marketplace,
scrap pile). Then hand him your phone with the site open. The question does the
selling; every builder has the shelf problem and nobody has named it for him.

**The shop owner.** Lead with the shelf and the following, not community. His
customers already follow his work and it does nothing for him. This gives the
shop a page: builds, the parts off them, the people watching. He sets the price,
buyer pays at pickup, **we never touch your money**, he pays nothing — buyer pays
5%, capped. That money line is what lands; everyone's been burned by a platform
that holds funds.

## 04 — The deal

**He gets:** founding garage status (permanent, and 25 means 25) · $0 to sell
forever, not an intro rate · your personal number, answered · first feature in
the launch push · a real say before it's public.

**He gives:** one build up (not-for-sale is fine) · two parts on the bench ·
one update a month (a photo and a sentence) · honest feedback, especially what
confused him · **one introduction** to the next builder who should be here.

That last one compounds: ten founding garages each bringing one good intro gets
you to twenty with no cold DMs, and referred builders arrive already trusting it.

## 05 — Onboarding

Gate on staging being real: photo upload proven, one test charge through, and
your own 30-minute walk done. Never put the first founding garage on a version
you haven't walked yourself.

1. Set his garage up **while he's on the phone with you.** Ten minutes of his confusion teaches more than any internal testing.
2. Build posted, real photos, the story so far. If the photos are bad, say so — founding garages set the bar everyone copies.
3. Two take-offs on the bench with him, three facts each, honest condition. Where he hesitates is a product bug.
4. Ask what confused him and write it down **verbatim** — your paraphrase loses the thing that mattered.
5. Ask for the introduction before you hang up. The yes is warmest in the first ten minutes.
6. Follow up in a week with something that changed because of him. That's what turns a signup into a founder.

## 06 — What kills this

- **Waiting for the site to be finished.** Recruiting starts now; onboarding starts at staging. Only the second date depends on engineering.
- **Chasing 100 instead of landing 25.** Empty garages make the site look abandoned.
- **Letting a reseller in "for volume."** Kills the premise permanently.
- **Sending a link without a conversation.** Links convert nobody; the link is what you send *after*.
- **Promising features to close a yes.** Every promise is a roadmap item you didn't choose.
- **Going quiet after signup.** Three weeks of silence is a churned user who hasn't noticed yet.

## 07 — The board

25 slots, filled by name — no name, no slot. Slot 1 is the owner's own garage:
it's the example every other founding garage gets shown.

**Pace:** five conversations a week reaches 25 in about five weeks — roughly the
time the team needs for Road to Nine plus staging. Run them in parallel and
launch day has a full site. Run them in sequence and launch day is an empty feed.
